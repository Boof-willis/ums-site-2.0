Once you’re here, why not check out Timpanogos Cave National Monument, a stunning natural wonder located in the Wasatch Range of northern Utah. Named after the Native American Ute word for "cave of the falling water," Timpanogos Cave is a three-level cave system with its own unique features, including hundreds of delicate mineral formations and an incredible variety of geological features. The entrance to this breathtaking underground world lies at an elevation of 6,700 feet and is surrounded by scenic alpine forests and meadows. The cave's passages were formed thousands of years ago by an underground river that eventually dried up and left behind intricate networks of limestone caverns. Visitors can explore two incredible levels of this sprawling cave system, admiring formations like stalactites, helictites, flowstone, draperies, soda straws, and many other unique rock structures along the way.
