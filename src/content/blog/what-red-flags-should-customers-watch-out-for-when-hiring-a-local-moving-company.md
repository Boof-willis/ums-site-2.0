Hiring a moving company should take stress off your plate, not add to it. But handing your belongings over to strangers can feel nerve-wracking, especially with so many companies competing for your business in Utah. Some are honest, experienced, and ready to look out for you. Others are cutting corners, and a few are outright scammers.

The good news is that bad movers almost always leave clues. Once you know what to look for, the warning signs are easy to spot, and the right questions will quickly separate the professionals from the pretenders. Here is everything you need to choose a moving company you can trust, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## Red Flags That Signal a Moving Scam

A legitimate company has a track record, real credentials, and nothing to hide. Watch for these warning signs as you research.

### No Verifiable Company Information

If a company has no website, or the site is bare with no physical address, licensing details, or proof of insurance, walk away. It does not matter how good the price sounds if you cannot verify who you are dealing with. Be suspicious if you call and get a generic greeting like "Moving Company" instead of an actual brand name. Look for an established business with a real presence in your area, ideally one that has served the community for years.

### Missing or Hidden Licensing

Running a moving company takes more than a big truck and strong backs. Reputable movers hold a valid business license and, for interstate moves, register with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA). Ask for their licensing information directly. Trustworthy companies share it without hesitation. A company that is licensed is not automatically perfect, but a company that is not licensed is an easy one to cross off your list.

### Suspiciously Low Bids

Scammers lure customers with prices that are dramatically lower than every other quote you collect. A lowball estimate often means cut corners on materials, care, or staff training, or it is bait for a classic hostage scam where the price balloons after your belongings are loaded. If a bid seems too good to be true, it usually is.

### No Written Estimate or Confirmation

Never settle for a verbal price. A legitimate company puts the estimate in writing and sends confirmation paperwork that includes your move date and the services included. That paperwork is your insurance policy against miscommunication and no-show crews. Any company unwilling to put its commitments in print is not your best moving partner.

### Promises That Insurance Covers Everything

Be wary of anyone who claims their coverage protects against every possible problem. Standard liability protection typically starts at 60 cents per pound, meaning movers reimburse at least 60 cents for every pound of a damaged or lost item. A company promising far more without the paperwork to back it up may be misleading you. Understand the policy before anything goes on the truck.

### Large Deposits or Cash-Only Payment

Reputable movers ask for a reasonable deposit, if any, and accept multiple payment methods such as credit cards and checks. Be cautious of companies that demand a large upfront payment, insist on cash only, or want substantial deposits during the slow season.

### Unmarked Trucks and Unprofessional Staff

Most established movers brand their vehicles and put their crews in clearly identifiable uniforms. A completely unmarked truck is a red flag. So is rude, pushy, or evasive behavior. Pay attention to demeanor. Your movers should be knowledgeable, courteous, and well trained.

### A Pattern of Negative Reviews

A single complaint happens to everyone. A consistent pattern of reports about damaged items, hidden fees, missed dates, or unprofessional conduct is a clear warning. Read reviews on Google, Yelp, My Moving Reviews, and the Better Business Bureau before you commit.

## Questions to Ask Before You Hire

Treat hiring movers like a job interview. Honest companies welcome these questions; the dishonest ones get defensive.

- **How much experience do you have with my specific type of move?** A 12th-floor penthouse move requires different equipment and planning than a single-family home in the suburbs. Make sure they have handled situations like yours.
- **Are you a moving company or a broker, and do you use subcontractors?** This matters more than most people realize (more on brokers below).
- **What liability coverage options do you offer, and can I see proof of insurance?** Every reputable mover carries multiple coverage options and will gladly show its policy.
- **Do you provide a binding written quote?** Understand whether your estimate is binding, non-binding, or a flat hourly rate, and what could change it.
- **Are there any additional or hidden charges?** Ask directly about add-ons, fuel and mileage fees, and surprise costs so there are none on moving day.
- **Do you run background checks on your employees?** Strangers will be in and out of your home all day. Knowing the crew is vetted brings real peace of mind.
- **How can I reach your company and crew during the move?** Save the contact info on your phone before the big day.
- **What payment methods do you accept?** Confirm in advance so you are not caught off guard when the bill arrives.
- **Do you offer the specific services I need?** Packing, storage, custom crating, assembly and disassembly, specialty items like pianos, and vehicle shipping all vary by company.

## Qualifications of an Exceptional Mover

Beyond avoiding the bad, here is what genuinely good movers have in common.

**Experience and the right resources.** Years in the business means they know how to protect your antique table and your grandmother's art. They also have the trucks, equipment, storage options, and proper documentation to tailor the move to your needs, including long-distance experience if your move calls for it.

**Strong communication.** A company that is hard to reach and vague when you finally connect will make moving day miserable. Responsiveness is one of the clearest signs of a professional.

**Reliability and reputation.** Reliable movers show up when they say they will and have the reviews and references to prove it. A certified, well-trained crew that follows safe handling and packing practices is a sign of real expertise.

**Transparency and efficiency.** Honest movers offer free estimates, explain how they calculate pricing, and never spring hidden fees on you. And after a week of packing, you will appreciate a crew that works quickly and carefully with no damage and no drama.

## Moving Companies vs. Moving Brokers

One distinction trips up a lot of first-time movers. A moving company owns its trucks and equipment and employs the crew that does the hands-on work from start to finish. A moving broker is a middleman who finds a mover for you and schedules the job, but does not touch your belongings.

In most cases, going straight to a moving company is the better choice. Brokers cannot answer detailed questions about your move, cannot negotiate prices or offer discounts, and add their fee on top of the mover's cost. They often operate out of distant call centers, so reaching a real person about your specific move can be frustrating. When you get an estimate directly from the company doing the work, it tends to be more accurate, too. If a company hesitates to tell you whether it is a broker or a carrier, treat that as a red flag.

## What Counts as a Scam, and What Does Not

Not every hiccup is fraud. Even excellent movers occasionally run into trouble. The following usually do not constitute a scam:

- Minor damage to belongings
- A small number of misplaced items
- A delivery delay of a day or two
- A cost increase of less than 10 percent due to an inaccurate estimate

Genuine scams look different and deserve to be reported:

- Creating a fraudulent bill of lading, the document outlining the destination, quantity, and type of items transported
- Quoting a suspiciously low price, then holding your belongings hostage until you pay far more
- Lying about the weight or volume of your shipment
- Continually piling on new charges

The FMCSA enforces a 110 percent rule: a mover cannot demand more than 110 percent of the original agreed-upon cost before delivering your items. If your final bill is wildly higher than your estimate, something is wrong.

## How to Report a Moving Scam

If you have been defrauded, act quickly.

1. **File a complaint with the company first.** A legitimate mover takes customer service seriously and will work toward a fair resolution. Contact the local office and, if needed, the headquarters.
2. **Escalate to the right agency.** If the company will not respond, file with the FMCSA (part of the US Department of Transportation), the American Trucking Association, the Better Business Bureau, or your state's moving-fraud agency.
3. **Consider legal action as a last resort.** If nothing else works, a trusted attorney can advise whether you have a valid claim for small claims court.

Even with a reputable company, you have every right to file a complaint over severely damaged items, major delays, or poor communication.
