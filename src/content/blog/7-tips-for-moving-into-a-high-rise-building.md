Moving into a high-rise building has a lot of perks. The incredible views, the many amenities, and the city feel, to name a few. Unfortunately, moving into one is not one of the highlights, but it has to be done. Your stuff has to get in there somehow. However, there are some ways to make the process go smoother. Here are seven tips to help you during your upcoming move into an enviable high-rise.

## 1. Don’t Forget to Declutter

Our first tip is always the same whether you’re upgrading or down-sizing your new home; declutter. Failing to declutter before moving is a rookie mistake. Decluttering feels so good. This step is especially pertinent when moving into a high-rise apartment or condo because there usually isn’t a garage, basement, or attic for storage.

Also, your belongings must travel up several floors to reach their destination. If your place doesn’t have an elevator, the move may motivate you to become a minimalist. The less you have, the less you need to haul up on a moving day.

## 2. Plan Furniture Placement in Advance

How awful would it be if you or your furniture movers hauled your oversized couch all the way up to your place only to find out that it doesn’t fit anywhere? It’s a good idea to plan these things out in advance.

Ask your building manager for a floor plan of your place with room dimensions. You don’t have to plan out every detail down to the last kitchen chair, but if you know the measurements, you can do some quick measuring on your larger pieces to ensure you won’t have a major dilemma on your hands. It’s also a good idea to measure doorways in the hallways and your apartment to ensure your furniture fits through them.

## 3. Know the Rules

Most apartment buildings and condo communities have rules and regulations that the tenants must follow regarding moving in and out. They may have restrictions on moving times. For instance, they may prohibit moving in after business hours. If there are elevators (for your sake, we sure hope they are), you may only be allowed to use a certain one for moving. Movers might be required to use a freight elevator or might be required to reserve a main elevator in advance. You may consider asking your moving company in Utah if they’ve made a move to your building before and if they know the rules and regulations.

## 4. Coordinate With Management

It’s common courtesy to notify your management beforehand when you’ll be moving in so they know. This allows them to be prepared for extra foot traffic and sometimes tell other residents about any extra noise. They may also give you some leeway on parking your moving truck, and they may give you some tips on how to make the move-in easier. Your managers will appreciate you letting them know, especially if they must coordinate with other incoming tenants. Having too many residents move into the same building may be unsafe, unproductive, and disruptive.

## 5. Hire Insured Movers

When you’re searching for moving companies in Utah, make sure to ask for proof of their insurance before hiring them. Most high-rise buildings require tenants to use professional moving companies that are adequately insured to protect their buildings against any damages. Many buildings won’t let their residents move in alone and require professional movers.

## 6. Take Extra Precautions

High-rise buildings can be tricky to move into. You don’t just go straight into your place; as easy as pie, like a ground-level home. You must bring your things through hallways and doors before moving into your personal unit. It’s wise to take safety precautions such as padding doorways, elevators, and edges you’ll be cutting through frequently to prevent damage to the building and your belongings. Utah’s Moving and Storage always takes every precaution, regardless of the residence you’re moving into.

## 7. Secure Parking Ahead of Time

Parking lots can be crowded with limited spots at high-rise buildings, especially for a massive moving truck. When talking to your manager about your upcoming move-in day, bring up the parking situation and ask where you can park the car and what would allow the most accessible access to your particular unit—secure parking spaces for any vehicles that will be part of your move.

Communication and coordination are essential for moving into a high-rise building or an apartment living situation. Moving to a high floor is a lot of work, so follow the proper steps to make it all go as smoothly as possible.
